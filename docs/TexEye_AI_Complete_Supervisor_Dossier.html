1.2 Why Adding More Features is Dangerous (Avoiding Scope Creep)

Scope Creep Warning: Adding automated cutting table actuators, high-voltage industrial PLCs, or full ERP accounting would risk student safety and graduation deadlines without adding academic AI value.

In industrial textile factories, automated cutting tables use 400V high-torque blade gantries costing millions of rupees and requiring specialized mechanical fabrication and high-voltage safety certifications. Attempting to fabricate physical fabric cutters in an undergraduate university lab introduces severe mechanical hazards and timeline delays that cause over 85% of multi-disciplinary hardware projects to fail their final evaluation.

TexEye AI targets the automated quality inspection and certification stage—the highest-value software and AI bottleneck in the textile manufacturing process. The prototype is fully self-contained, safe, scientifically rigorous, and completely achievable within the two-semester timeline.

3. Operational Breakdown: How Each Module Works ("Knsa Module Kese Kam Krega") Step-by-Step Flow

The complete TexEye AI system operates through 5 tightly coordinated modules. Below is the exact step-by-step technical and operational narrative of how each module functions during fabric roll inspection.

Module 1: AI Defect Detection & Location Module (Owner: Muhammad Bilal Butt)

Goal: Detect fabric flaws in real-time, determine exact physical spatial coordinates without guessing, and ensure zero duplicate defect records.

  • Step 1 (Frame Ingestion): The industrial camera continuously feeds 30 to 60 frames per second to the edge inference engine on the stand PC.
  • Step 2 (YOLO Inference): A lightweight, fine-tuned YOLO model processes each frame. It outputs defect bounding boxes [u_min, v_min, u_max, v_max], detection confidence (e.g. 0.88), and predicted class (Hole, Oil Stain, Foreign Yarn, Slub).
  • Step 3 (Metric Coordinate Localization): Unlike toy systems that only know pixel numbers, Module 1 reads the optical rotary encoder pulse count at the exact moment the frame was captured. It translates pulses into physical metric coordinates:
    • Longitudinal length along the roll: Y_meters = (pulse_count × mm_per_pulse) / 1000.
    • Transverse position across the fabric width: X_cm = pixel_u_center × (fabric_width_cm / frame_width_pixels).
  • Step 4 (Multi-Frame Kalman Deduplication): As fabric rolls at ~0.3–0.5 m/s, a single 15-cm defect appears across 10 to 20 consecutive camera frames. A 2D Kalman filter with Hungarian IoU tracking tracks the defect trajectory across frames. When the defect exits the camera view, it commits exactly ONE deduplicated record with its true physical length.
  • Step 5 (Defect Crop Extraction): Extracts a 256×256 high-resolution JPEG crop centered on the flaw and saves it to local NVMe storage with a unique UUID filename.

Module 2: Hardware & Inspection Monitoring Module (Owner: Muhammad Bilal Butt)

Goal: Guarantee inspection reliability through environmental safety invariants, trip physical relay alarms, and flag uninspected fabric spans.

  • Step 1 (Pre-Inference Health Sentinel): Before any frame is sent to YOLO, Module 2 executes two instant safety checks:
    • Luminance Guard: Computes mean image brightness (Lux). If lux falls below 800 (dim light/LED failure), the invariant is violated.
    • Focus Sharpness Guard: Computes the Laplacian variance. If the variance falls below threshold (motion blur or dirty lens), the invariant is violated.
    • Frame Drop Sentinel: Monitors camera buffer timestamp gaps. If fabric travels >15 mm without a frame, dropped frames are logged.
  • Step 2 (Hardware Alarm Actuation): Upon invariant breach, the stand PC immediately triggers a USB-connected relay module. The relay trips a physical Amber Stack Light and audible buzzer mounted on the stand, alerting the factory operator.
  • Step 3 (Uninspected Span Recording): AI inference is immediately suspended to prevent false negatives. The current encoder length is logged as Y_start = 14.2m in SQLite table uninspected_spans.
  • Step 4 (Recovery & Flagging): When lighting or focus is restored, the relay turns OFF, and Y_end = 15.8m is recorded. This span [14.2m – 15.8m] is marked as UNINSPECTED on the roll's digital record. The operator is later forced to re-inspect this section, guaranteeing 100% quality compliance.

Module 3: Mobile Floor Application (Owner: Muhammad Afnan Khan)

Goal: Provide the shop-floor operator with an intuitive, mobile touch interface for session setup, real-time speed monitoring, and tactile vibration defect alerts.

  • Step 1 (Session Initiation via QR Scan): The operator opens the Flutter app on an Android tablet mounted to the stand frame. Using the device camera, the operator scans the QR code printed on the raw fabric roll tag.
  • Step 2 (Stand Sync): The app transmits roll metadata (Roll ID, Fabric Type, Target Length) to the edge inspection stand and initializes the run.
  • Step 3 (Live Telemetry View): During inspection, the app displays a dynamic dashboard showing current fabric velocity (m/s), elapsed inspection meters, and cumulative defect count via a local low-latency WebSocket.
  • Step 4 (Haptic Defect Alert): When Module 1 detects a critical flaw (e.g. a hole > 25 mm), the backend pushes an instant WebSocket event. The mobile device triggers a haptic vibration pulse (500ms buzz) and pops up a high-contrast alert modal, ensuring the operator notices even in a noisy weaving shed.
  • Step 5 (Floor Tagging): If the operator spots a rare manual issue (e.g. selvage curl or oil drip from stand gears), they tap "Add Floor Tag" to log the defect location into the session.

Module 4: Web Quality Management & Digital Fabric Map (Owner: Muhammad Afnan Khan)

Goal: Give the quality inspector and QA team a comprehensive desktop control room to audit fabric rolls, view the 2D unwound fabric map, zoom into flaw crops, and reclassify defects with audit trails.

  • Step 1 (Batch Ingestion & Storage): When the stand PC is online, its transactional outbox pushes completed roll defect batches and high-res crops over HTTPS REST to the central FastAPI backend. Data is committed into PostgreSQL and MinIO object storage.
  • Step 2 (2D Canvas Digital Roll Map): The inspector opens the React web dashboard. The system renders an interactive HTML5 Canvas 2D Digital Fabric Map representing the entire unwound fabric roll (e.g. 100 meters long by 1.8 meters wide). Flaws are plotted as interactive colored glyphs at their exact (X cm, Y m) coordinates, with amber bands highlighting any uninspected spans.
  • Step 3 (Zoomable Crop Gallery & Electronic Calipers): Clicking any flaw glyph opens an inspection drawer showing the high-resolution 256×256 JPEG crop. The inspector can zoom in and drag calibrated digital calipers across the image to independently verify defect millimeter dimensions.
  • Step 4 (Role-Based Defect Reclassification): If the AI model misclassified an oil stain as foreign yarn, the inspector selects the correct class from a dropdown, enters an audit reason, and saves. The system updates the defect record and creates an immutable log in defect_audit_logs recording inspector ID, old class, new class, and timestamp.

Module 5: Smart Operations & ASTM 4-Point Grading (Joint Collaboration)

Goal: Automate international export quality grading using the ASTM D5430-07 standard and generate cryptographically verified PDF quality certificates.

  • Step 1 (ASTM D5430-07 Penalty Assignment): When inspection completes, Module 5 retrieves all verified defects for the roll and applies standard 4-point penalty rules based on physical length:
    • Flaw length up to 3 inches (75 mm) = 1 penalty point.
    • Flaw length over 3 to 6 inches (150 mm) = 2 penalty points.
    • Flaw length over 6 to 9 inches (230 mm) = 3 penalty points.
    • Flaw length over 9 inches (230 mm) = 4 penalty points.
    • Any hole larger than 1 inch (25 mm) = 4 penalty points (instant severe penalty).
    • Maximum penalty per linear meter is capped at 4 points.
  • Step 2 (Roll Quality Score Calculation): Calculates the standardized points per 100 square meters:
    Score = (Total Penalty Points × 100) / (Roll Length in meters × Fabric Width in meters).
  • Step 3 (Automated Grade Assignment): Compares the calculated score against configured factory thresholds:
    • Score ≤ 20 points/100m² → Grade A (Export Quality).
    • Score between 21 and 35 points/100m² → Grade B (Domestic Quality).
    • Score > 35 points/100m² → Scrap / Downgraded.
  • Step 4 (Cryptographically Sealed PDF Certificate): The Quality Manager reviews the scorecard and clicks "Export Official Certificate". The system compiles a publication-ready PDF containing roll metadata, defect density chart, 2D map miniature, inspector audit notes, and a SHA-256 digital signature seal that buyers can verify via QR code to prevent counterfeit textile inspection certificates.

13.2 Top 10 Anticipated Tough Supervisor Questions & Winning Answers

Q1 Supervisor: "Why do you need an optical rotary encoder? Can't you calculate defect location from video frame rate?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, in real textile stands, fabric velocity is never perfectly constant—motors experience slippage, gradual acceleration, and deceleration. If we relied on frame rate alone, coordinate errors would compound, and a flaw at 50 meters would be mislocated by 2 to 5 meters. The optical rotary encoder directly contacts the roller, providing physical pulse counts that give millimeter-exact coordinates regardless of motor speed variations."
Q2 Supervisor: "What happens if a single defect is visible in 15 consecutive frames? Won't your AI count it as 15 defects?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, that is precisely why Bilal has implemented a 2D Kalman filter multi-frame tracker in Module 1. As the defect moves across consecutive frames, Hungarian IoU association tracks the flaw's bounding box trajectory. Only after the defect completely exits the camera view is a single deduplicated defect record committed with its true integrated physical length."
Q3 Supervisor: "What happens if factory Wi-Fi disconnects while a 500-meter roll is being inspected?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, inspection continues with zero interruption. TexEye AI employs an offline-first architecture. The stand PC writes defect records and high-res crops directly to local SQLite WAL storage. Our transactional Outbox daemon monitors connectivity and automatically syncs all queued batches to the central FastAPI backend once Wi-Fi reconnects, with zero data loss and zero production stoppage."
Q4 Supervisor: "What if the inspection stand lighting dims or a bulb fails during production?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, that is handled by Module 2's Hardware Reliability Sentinel. Before running AI, we compute frame luminance. If lux drops below 800, a USB relay immediately trips a physical amber stack light and siren to warn the operator. Concurrently, the system suspends AI and logs the span [Y_start, Y_end] as UNINSPECTED in SQLite. Defective fabric is never passed off as inspected."
Q5 Supervisor: "Why did you build both a mobile app and a web dashboard? Isn't one interface enough?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, they serve two fundamentally different operational environments and users. The Floor Operator works beside the noisy vibrating machine stand, needing a portable, touch-optimized mobile app with camera QR scanning and haptic buzzers. The Quality Inspector and QA Manager work in an office environment, requiring a desktop web dashboard with a high-resolution 2D canvas roll map, zoomable calipers, and PDF export tools."
Q6 Supervisor: "What if your AI model misclassifies a defect? Can a human inspector override it?"
Winning Answer: "Yes, sir. In Module 4, the Quality Inspector can open any flaw crop, review it with calibrated calipers, and reclassify it. However, to prevent fraud, the system enforces an immutable audit trail in PostgreSQL: the original AI prediction, the inspector's name, the new category, the mandatory reason, and the timestamp are permanently logged."
Q7 Supervisor: "Is the ASTM D5430 4-point system an official international standard or did you invent it?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, ASTM D5430 is the globally recognized American standard for fabric inspection used across worldwide textile export supply chains. It assigns 1 to 4 penalty points based strictly on flaw length, caps penalties at 4 points per meter, and standardizes total points per 100 square meters. By adhering strictly to ASTM D5430-07, TexEye AI produces genuine commercial grade certificates accepted by international textile buyers."
Q8 Supervisor: "How do you prevent someone from tampering with or falsifying the exported PDF certificate?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, in Module 5, whenever a certificate is finalized, the backend computes a cryptographic SHA-256 hash digest of the entire roll inspection record and embeds it both textually and as a QR verification seal. Anyone scanning the QR code is directed to our central API endpoint, which validates whether the certificate matches the immutable database record."
Q9 Supervisor: "Should we add automated robotic fabric cutters to your project?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, we thoroughly evaluated that during requirements engineering. Automated cutting tables require 400V industrial electrical supply, complex pneumatic actuators, and mechanical safety cages that exceed lab safety limits and undergraduate FYP budgets. Our focus is automated computer vision inspection and quality certification—which is the primary AI and data science bottleneck in modern Pakistani textile mills."
Q10 Supervisor: "What is the exact division of work between Bilal and Afnan?"
Winning Answer: "Sir, Muhammad Bilal Butt owns the Edge AI and Hardware domain: Module 1 (Dataset curation, YOLO vision inference, encoder pulse-to-metric localization, and Kalman tracking) and Module 2 (Hardware reliability invariants, USB relay actuation, and uninspected span tracking). Muhammad Afnan Khan owns the Full-Stack and Operational domain: Module 3 (Flutter mobile floor app with QR scanning and WebSocket haptics) and Module 4 (React web dashboard, 2D HTML5 canvas fabric roll map, and audit logs). Module 5 (ASTM 4-point grading and signed PDF export) is our joint integration milestone."
