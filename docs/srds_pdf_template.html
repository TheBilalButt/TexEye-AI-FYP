1 Introduction

1.1 System Introduction

TexEye AI is an industrial edge-cloud hybrid automated fabric defect inspection and digitized quality management system engineered specifically for high-throughput textile manufacturing mills. Operating downstream of fabric weaving looms and unrolling stands, TexEye AI consumes continuous high-resolution optical video streams synchronized directly with physical mill movement via a hardware rotary optical encoder. Rather than relying on cloud-dependent vision APIs that introduce latency and network fragility, TexEye AI executes real-time defect segmentation, classification, multi-frame Kalman tracking, and physical millimeter metrology locally on an industrial edge computing unit (NVIDIA Jetson / Linux x86 edge rig) stationed directly beside the inspection table.

The system identifies and categorizes the primary structural and superficial fabric defects encountered in Pakistani textile production—including broken warp threads, broken weft picks, oil streaks, holes, floats, dirty spots, and stitching irregularities. Concurrently, it maintains a strict mathematical invariant: fabric sections subjected to optical degradation, camera drops, or speed violations are explicitly flagged as Uninspected Sections and forbidden from being certified clean. Verified inspection batches are stored in an offline-resilient local SQLite Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) outbox queue and pushed automatically to an enterprise cloud backend (FastAPI, PostgreSQL, MinIO) whenever network connectivity permits, enabling executive quality analytics, interactive 2D defect heatmaps, and automated ASTM D5430-07 4-point penalty grading.

1.2 Background of the System

In Pakistan's industrial textile sector—centered around major weaving hubs in Wah, Taxila, Rawalpindi, and Faisalabad—fabric quality assurance remains overwhelmingly manual. Human inspectors stand over motorized rolling frames where fabric moves continuously at linear velocities between 0.2 and 0.5 meters per second (12 to 30 meters per minute). Human physiological limits, eye fatigue, and visual accommodation fatigue cause defect escape rates of 30% to 40% after just 45 to 60 minutes of uninterrupted work.

Furthermore, traditional manual inspection produces handwritten paper roll logs that completely fail to record exact metric coordinates $(X_{\text{lateral}}, Y_{\text{longitudinal}})$. Inspectors mark defects using temporary chalk or paper stickers that regularly fall off during winding, packing, and shipment. The resulting disconnect between shop-floor quality inspection and computerized Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) databases causes costly fabric rejections at international export ports and leaves downstream garment cutting rooms unable to optimize computerized marker placement around known flaws. Existing commercial automated optical inspection (AOI) machines are imported proprietary monoliths costing tens of thousands of dollars, completely inaccessible to small-to-medium Pakistani weaving units. TexEye AI solves this critical national challenge by delivering an accessible, open-standard, edge-first AI inspection ecosystem tailored to local industrial realities.

1.3 Objectives of the System

  • Real-Time Edge Optical Ingestion: Capture continuous area-scan video at ≥ 30 frames per second at 1080p resolution, synchronized with mill fabric speed via hardware GPIO quadrature pulses.
  • Millimeter-Accurate Physical Metrology: Map localized pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ to true physical coordinates $(X_{\text{mm}}, Y_{\text{meters}})$ on the moving fabric web, independent of motor acceleration or slippage.
  • High-Precision Defect Classification & Localization: Fine-tune single-stage convolutional detection models (YOLOv8/v11) to achieve ≥ 88% mean Average Precision ($mAP@0.5$) across standard textile defect categories under TensorRT FP16 acceleration (< 25 ms inference latency per frame).
  • Multi-Frame Tracking & Deduplication: Associate defect bounding boxes across sequential overlapping frames using a Kalman Filter and IoU tracker (ByteTrack) to eliminate duplicate counts of traversing flaws.
  • Non-Negotiable Reliability Invariant Enforcement: Continuously monitor camera frame delivery, illumination lux levels, and image sharpness; immediately flag degraded spans as UNINSPECTED_SECTION to mathematically guarantee that uninspected fabric is never falsely graded clean.
  • Automated ASTM D5430-07 4-Point Grading: Calculate standardized penalty points (1 to 4 points based on defect length and direction) per 100 square meters, automatically assigning commercial Roll Grades (Grade A, Grade B, Grade C, or Reject).
  • Offline-Resilient Transactional Outbox: Persist all frame metadata and defect records to a local SQLite WAL buffer, guaranteeing zero inspection data loss during mill network disconnections, with auto-resume HTTPS batch synchronization.
  • Shop-Floor Audio-Visual Alarming & Dual UI: Actuate a physical 24V industrial stack light and buzzer via opto-isolated relay on critical defects, while serving real-time alerts to a Flutter floor mobile app and an executive React web dashboard.

1.4 Significance of the System

TexEye AI delivers immediate measurable significance across three fundamental operational dimensions:

  • Industrial & Economic Impact: Eliminates manual visual fatigue bottlenecks, reducing fabric defect escape rates from 35% down to under 5%. Automating the ASTM 4-point calculation eliminates human grading bias, protects Pakistani textile mills against international chargebacks, and cuts annual post-export rejection claims.
  • Cutting-Room Optimization (Industry 4.0): By storing high-precision spatial coordinates $(X, Y)$ for every flaw in a digital database, garment manufacturing cut-plans can automatically arrange CAD marker patterns to avoid flawed areas, converting otherwise rejected fabric into usable sub-panels.
  • Academic & Engineering Rigor: Demonstrates a production-grade cyber-physical system integrating hardware interrupt drivers, opto-isolated industrial relays, TensorRT-accelerated edge AI, formal state-machine fault tolerance, and offline-first distributed cloud architectures in a real-world BS-AI capstone.

2 Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective

TexEye AI is a complete, self-contained cyber-physical quality assurance ecosystem designed to operate directly within textile weaving, knitting, and fabric inspection lines. The system does not attempt to replace mechanical rolling tables; rather, it mounts non-invasively onto existing motorized inspection frames (e.g., COTS inspection frames commonly found in Wah and Taxila weaving mills).

The physical environment consists of an extruded aluminum structural arch spanning across the width of the fabric table (typically 1.6 to 2.2 meters wide). An overhead industrial area-scan camera (USB 3.0 / GigE) and a regulated high-CRI LED lighting bar are rigidly suspended above the moving fabric. Below, a roller-coupled incremental rotary optical encoder transmits quadrature pulses into the hardware GPIO pins of an industrial edge computing unit, generating hardware interrupts that trigger frame captures synchronously with physical linear web displacement.

Internally, TexEye AI implements a decoupled two-tier architecture:

  • Industrial Edge Tier: Stationed at the machine stand, running Linux, OpenCV, TensorRT, and a lightweight edge daemon. It handles high-frequency physical ingestion (30+ FPS), metric coordinate mapping, YOLO inference, Kalman defect tracking, stack-light relay actuation, reliability invariant checks, and local persistence in a transactional SQLite (WAL) buffer.
  • Central Cloud / Enterprise Tier: Hosted on a centralized server or cloud instance (FastAPI, PostgreSQL, MinIO S3, Redis). It consumes validated inspection batches from the edge daemons via HTTPS REST endpoints, computes enterprise ASTM D5430 roll grading metrics, manages role-based access control (RBAC), and serves WebSocket push notifications to floor mobile devices and web workstation clients.

2.3 Product Functionality

The major functional capabilities of TexEye AI are summarized below and fully specified in Chapter 3:

  • Roll Initialization: Floor operators scan a physical QR code on the raw fabric roll to bind roll metadata (Batch ID, Fabric Construction, Target Length, GSM, Warp/Weft Density) into the local inspection session.
  • Synchronized Optical Capture: Camera frame acquisition is triggered synchronously with optical encoder pulses, eliminating motion blur and geometric distortion across varying machine speeds.

  • Real-Time Defect Detection: YOLO deep convolutional inference evaluates each captured frame, outputting bounding box coordinates, defect class ID, and confidence score within 25 ms.
  • Physical Coordinate Metrology: The system transforms image bounding box pixels into real-world coordinates: lateral position $X$ (0 to web width in mm) and longitudinal position $Y$ (continuous linear meters based on encoder pulses).
  • Flaw Deduplication & Tracking: A multi-frame Kalman Filter associates detections across consecutive frames to track continuous defects (e.g., warp streaks) and prevent redundant counts.
  • Automatic Alarm Actuation: When severe defects (holes > 50 mm, dense defect clusters) are detected, the edge unit drives an opto-isolated relay triggering a 24V DC red stack light and buzzer.
  • Inspection Invariant Guard: If illumination drops below 800 lux, frame rate falls below 25 FPS, or image blur exceeds tolerance, the system marks the segment as UNINSPECTED_SECTION.
  • Offline Outbox Persistence: Inspection events and defect records are committed locally to SQLite WAL tables, buffering up to 500,000 frames during network outages.
  • Cloud Batch Synchronization: A background sync daemon queries unsynced records, packs them into multipart HTTPS payloads with JPEG crops, and pushes them to the central FastAPI server.
  • ASTM D5430-07 Grading: The central engine evaluates defect lengths and orientations, computes penalty points per 100 square meters, and assigns official roll grades (A, B, C, Reject).
  • Dual-Client Interaction: Floor operators receive vibration alerts on mobile devices; quality managers inspect interactive 2D defect roll maps, adjust labels, and export signed PDF certificates.

procedure PROCESS_EDGE_INSPECTION_FRAME(frame_item)
    // Step 1: Execute TensorRT FP16 YOLO Inference
    detections ← TENSORRT_YOLO_ENGINE.INFER(frame_item.image_buffer)
    
    // Step 2: Multi-Frame Kalman Association
    KALMAN_TRACKER.PREDICT_ALL_TRACKS()
    matched_tracks, unmatched_dets, lost_tracks ← KALMAN_TRACKER.HUNGARIAN_MATCH(detections, IOU_THRESHOLD=0.35)
    
    for each (track, det) in matched_tracks do
        track.UPDATE(det.bbox, det.confidence, frame_item.y_meters)
        if track.consecutive_hits ≥ 3 and not track.is_confirmed then
            track.is_confirmed ← TRUE
        end if
    end for
    
    for each det in unmatched_dets do
        KALMAN_TRACKER.INIT_NEW_TRACK(det, frame_item.y_meters)
    end for
    
    // Step 3: Finalize closed tracks exiting the field of view
    for each track in lost_tracks do
        if track.is_confirmed and track.frames_lost > MAX_LOST_FRAMES then
            defect_record ← COMPUTE_PHYSICAL_METROLOGY(track, frame_item)
            LOCAL_OUTBOX.INSERT_DEFECT(defect_record)
            if defect_record.penalty_points ≥ 4 then
                TRIGGER_RELAY_ALARM(SEVERITY_CRITICAL)
            end if
            KALMAN_TRACKER.DELETE_TRACK(track)
        end if
    end for
end procedure

procedure COMPUTE_PHYSICAL_METROLOGY(track, current_frame)

    u_center ← (track.bbox.u_min + track.bbox.u_max) / 2.0

                             (track.bbox.v_max - track.bbox.v_min) × CALIBRATION_KY_MM_PER_PIXEL)

        class_label: track.majority_class,
        confidence: track.mean_confidence,

procedure COMPUTE_ASTM_ROLL_GRADE(roll_id, defects_list, uninspected_list, roll_length_m, fabric_width_m)
    // Non-Negotiable Invariant Check
    if uninspected_list.LENGTH > 0 then
        return { grade: "REJECT", reason: "CONTAINS_UNINSPECTED_SECTIONS", points_per_100sqm: NULL }
    end if
    
    // Partition roll into 1-meter longitudinal bins
    meter_bins ← ARRAY(SIZE = CEIL(roll_length_m), INITIAL_VALUE = 0)
    for each defect in defects_list do
        bin_index ← FLOOR(defect.y_meters)
        meter_bins[bin_index] ← meter_bins[bin_index] + defect.penalty_points
    end for
    
    // Enforce ASTM D5430 Rule: Maximum 4 points per linear meter
    total_capped_points ← 0
    for each meter_points in meter_bins do
        total_capped_points ← total_capped_points + MIN(meter_points, 4)
    end for
    
    // Normalize to points per 100 square meters
    total_area_sqm ← roll_length_m × fabric_width_m
    points_per_100sqm ← (total_capped_points × 100.0) / total_area_sqm
    
    // Assign Commercial Roll Grade
    if points_per_100sqm ≤ 20.0 then grade ← "GRADE_A"
    else if points_per_100sqm ≤ 28.0 then grade ← "GRADE_B"
    else if points_per_100sqm ≤ 40.0 then grade ← "GRADE_C"
    else grade ← "REJECT"
    
    return { grade: grade, total_points: total_capped_points, points_per_100sqm: points_per_100sqm }
end procedure
